# Interview 06 — Lizzie Fluke, Director of Performance
**~2 hours across multiple sessions.** Segment: governance / expert informant.
Evidence: meeting summaries + short recordings (full recording not retained — no storage).
Product was demonstrated in session 2.

## 1. The core finding: the digital layer is missing
Saudi has mature national digital infrastructure in health and government — citizens get alerts
and medical records on their phones. **Sport has nothing equivalent.**

- **Data is fragmented across paper, Google Drive, Google Sheets, Excel, email and WhatsApp.**
  Many teams have no centralised platform at all.
- **There is no centralised data ownership.**
- **Athlete monitoring and electronic medical record systems are not established** in Saudi sport.
- Ministry of Health digital systems are the model sport should follow.

This is the delivery-layer argument confirmed from the inside, and it is far worse than
"clubs need better analytics" — **the substrate does not exist.**

## 2. The Saudi paradox
**Significant financial resources, missing operational foundations.** No standardised emergency
action procedures. No performance regulations of the kind the UK mandates. The Saudi Pro League
is working toward these but has not yet established centralised standards or compliance
requirements.

Capability varies enormously between federations. Her example: **the Taekwondo Federation —
excellent athletes, weak systems-led governance.** Federations sit at different development
levels and need tailored support, not a one-size-fits-all product.

## 3. ⭐ Sporting directors hold the power — and often lack the expertise
**The single most important finding for our ICP.**

- **Sporting directors in Saudi Arabia have significant power — more than in other regions.**
- **Decisions flow top-down through a vertical approval system** from sporting directors and
  higher management.
- **But they often lack experience in performance and medical management**, which produces
  real damage: poor scheduling, high injury rates, matches followed by late flights and
  morning training, players refusing contracts.

**Her advice on how to work with this:** do not fight the vertical system. **Work within it,
and add multidisciplinary "circles" around the athlete** — integrated teams rather than
isolated departments, with the athlete at the centre so the burden of connecting departments
does not fall on them.

**What this means for us:** the buyer is confirmed as the sporting director — they hold the
power. But they need the product to *carry* the expertise, because they may not have it
themselves. That is the KEMO argument, from an entirely independent direction.

## 4. The overwhelmed analyst is regional, not local
She confirmed directly that the pattern we found at Al-Hilal is **common across the region, not
specific to one club**: one staff member carrying multiple responsibilities on a limited budget
and with no support, producing a **"survival mindset — focused only on basic tasks rather than
innovation."**

Our single-interview finding is a regional condition.

## 5. ⭐ The metric-that-misled story — our thesis, with consequences
She described a head coach who used **total distance covered as the primary performance metric**.
Consequences:
- **Teams began making unnecessary runs to inflate their numbers.**
- **The coach made contract decisions on that basis** — decisions she describes as inappropriate.

Her position: **total distance should not be the primary metric; intensity metrics matter far
more** — high-speed running, accelerations, decelerations, braking, metres per minute.

**This is the single best illustration we have of "separating the needed metrics from the
noise."** It is not a marketing claim — it is a documented case where the wrong metric distorted
player behaviour and cost people contracts. Use it.

## 6. Context is everything
- Analyse **only players who played the full match** when computing totals; substitutes need
  separate treatment (different tactical instructions, limited minutes).
- **Player role and position change what the numbers mean** — the same figure reads differently
  for different players.
- **Pitch dimensions affect both load and tactical planning** and are routinely ignored.
- **Data literacy is needed across departments**, not only in the analyst.

## 7. Her own practice — the benchmark
At Malmö FF she built a **front-end GPS dashboard delivered within 90 minutes of a session
ending**, using Catapult data processed through Excel into Google Looker dashboards. Physical
data combined with video for tactical and physical analysis together.

**Her recommendation to us: integrate GPS and physical data with tactical and event data.**
That is a product direction, and it is also the clearest signal of where she sees a partnership.

## 8. Reaction to the product
Shown the working MVP — computer-vision extraction of performance metrics from video, player
comparison, progress tracking, multiple visualisations. **She loved it**, and asked for a
further session to test its capabilities.

**She offered to recommend people in Saudi football clubs for product feedback** — i.e. the
introductions. She also suggested connecting with the Al-Hilal U18 analyst (already done).

## 9. Ownership and funding — how adoption actually happens
Responsibility for integrating data tools sits with **both federations and clubs** — pathway
ownership is shared. In the UK, the national governing body supports clubs through mechanisms
like the **Workforce Fund** to raise standards in medical care and training.

**Implication: a federation-level funding mechanism is a plausible route to club adoption in
Saudi**, and it would bypass the individual club budget question entirely.

## 10. Follow-ups

  regarding training sports and exercise medicine doctors. A potential institutional contact.
- **She will provide names of people in Saudi clubs for product feedback.** Chase this — it is
