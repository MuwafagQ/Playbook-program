## 1. Role and responsibilities `[→ ICP: Who]`
U18 analyst at Al-Hilal. Responsibilities:
- Record matches and training sessions
- Discuss feedback with the coach
- Supply whatever the coach asks for
- Photoshop (graphics and visual materials)
- **Does not control budget.**

## 2. The story — how a match actually gets analysed `[→ JTBD: Context / Where they are now]`
- Analysis starts at **training**: set up cameras, then join the coach on the touchline for
  discussion.
- On match day the same: set-up first; a technical colleague prepares clips and notes for the
  coach; the analyst stands and observes the game.
- **One technical colleague was added recently.** During the match the analyst sits and watches.
- **It takes a whole day**, from preparation through set-up. The coach often asks for many
  extra tasks — highlighting a player, marking specific actions.
- Two output types: **classroom sessions** and **training sessions** — corrections and
  improvements. **Individual sessions barely happen — video only.**

## 3. Current solution `[→ ICP: Current Solution / JTBD: Habit / Value prop: Unlike]`
- **Veo camera** for training, with its Performance software. A separate camera and software
  for match analysis.
- **All of it is video and Photoshop. No data interaction at all.**
- Laptops; vests for the medical team.
- **Some analysts hold Hudl subscriptions.**
- **He is the only person working with data and video on the tactical side.**
- Doesn't know the costs. Mentioned **freelance tools around 300k**.
- *(What they tried and stopped — not recalled.)*

## 4. Pain `[→ ICP: Pain / JTBD: Push]`
- **"Time, time, time."** The dominant answer.
- **Lack of recognition.** The role has always been seen as not needed. He runs the analysis
  alone. He has asked repeatedly for another hire and got no response.
- Spoke **with visible pain** about the senior first team: *the first-team coach has a whole
  **team** of analysts.* He read that as reflecting workload rather than output.
- **It emerged he doesn't know much about data**, and Muwafag stopped probing — *he sensed
  embarrassment on the topic.*
- The coach **relies heavily on video**, so theory and training sessions suffer.
- *"Management will not care too much, since I'm attached to the coach."*
- **Emotional state: overloaded, worried about his position and lack of support, and
  embarrassed at not engaging with data.**
- *(A specific bad decision — not recalled.)*

## 5. Desired outcome `[→ JTBD: Desired outcome / ICP: Success Metric]`
- **Save time.** Keep up with the technology. Be resourced with a data team.
- **"It would work if it saved time and brought valuable insights."** ← his success metric

## 6. The three jobs `[→ JTBD canvas]`
- **Functional** — anything that saves time and effort and **is not complicated to use.**
  Most Photoshop-based solutions are very complicated.
- **Emotional** — wants to feel he did the job properly, to be **heard and recognised by
  management**, to deliver strong insights and to develop.
  *He showed little curiosity about data itself — he kept returning to the better-resourced
  teams.* The desire is for **status and support**, not for analytics.
- **Social** — Al-Hilal is already seen as a top club with top talent. He wanted extra
  resources to do the job justice. The time pressure comes from **having to always look
  perfect** because they are a top team.
  *Muwafag's read: a sense of "do the job or lose your position."*

## 7. How he thinks it gets solved
- Hire a team; better tools; make things easier for the coaches first.
- **"A lot of startups knock the door but never come again."** ← the most important sentence
  in this interview

## 8. New tools and trust `[→ JTBD: Anxiety / Habit]`
- **They need to be educated about new tools — and at the same time have no time to learn.**
- Lots of noise: things seen on social media, things recommended by colleagues.
- **His three trust criteria: adaptability, sustainability, education.**
- **Network effect / "legacy worshippers"** — the existing system is already operating and
  new products are not trusted. *(He didn't say this outright; Muwafag inferred it.)*
- Would he personally want it? **Yes** — but he didn't grasp the data topic well, so the
  deeper analytics were never discussed.

## 9. Buying process `[→ ICP: who signs]`
- **Not his responsibility.**
- Has never tried to recommend a tool — **"no time to research."**

## 10. Money `[→ ICP: Budget Signal]`

# Assignment fields

**Form Q7 — Main problem:**
`Interview 1 – Performance analyst, elite club U18: A full working day is consumed per match
by camera set-up, clipping and Photoshop, run single-handed, leaving no time for actual
analysis and no capacity to engage with data at all.`

**Form Q8 — Key insight:**
`Interview 1 – Performance analyst: The user's job to be done is getting his day back and
being taken seriously by management — not better metrics. He is a game analyst, not a data
analyst, and could not evaluate advanced analytics if offered. Anything sold to this persona
must save time first and teach as it works.`

**Interest:** MAYBE — enthusiastic, but not the buyer and unable to assess the core product.

**Best quotes:**
- *"Time, time, time."*
- *"A lot of startups knock the door but never come again."*
- *"They are game analysts, not data analysts."*
- *"Management will not care too much, since I'm attached to the coach."*

**What surprised us:**
That an analyst at one of the biggest clubs in Asia works alone, with **no data interaction
whatsoever** — only video and Photoshop. And that when data came up he became embarrassed
rather than interested. The gap is not that good tools don't exist; it is that **nobody in
this role has the time, training or standing to use them.**

**What this changes:**
1. **Time saved is the value proposition for the user, not insight.** Insight is the buyer's
   language; time is the user's.
2. **The product must teach while it works.** "They need education and have no time for it"
   is a product requirement, not a marketing problem. It validates plain-language
   notifications over dashboards.
3. **Vendor longevity is a purchase criterion.** "Startups knock and never come again" names
   the anxiety force exactly — adaptability, sustainability, education are his stated trust
   tests, and we must answer all three explicitly.
4. **Simplicity beats depth for this persona.** "Photoshop solutions are very complicated."
5. **Al-Hilal is not our ICP** — a top-tier club, and player sales sit in a different
   department, so the Verified Asset Ledger has no buyer here. Valuable as a contrast case:
   *even at the top of Saudi football, the U18 analyst has no data capability at all.* That is
   a strong line for the pitch.
6. **Follow up on PFSA and معهد إعداد القادة** — offered as partnership routes.
