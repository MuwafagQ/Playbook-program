# 1 — Performance analyst, Al-Hilal U18 (USER)

**Their world:** Records matches and training, discusses feedback with the coach, supplies
whatever the coach asks for, does Photoshop graphics. No budget control.

**Current solution:** Veo camera with Performance software for training; separate camera and
software for matches. **All video and Photoshop — no data interaction at all.** Some analysts
hold Hudl subscriptions. He is the only person working on video and tactics.

**Pain:** *"Time, time, time."* A full day per match, run single-handed. Has asked repeatedly
for another hire and been refused. Spoke with visible pain about the senior team's coach having
a whole *team* of analysts. **Became embarrassed when data came up** — he does not work with
data and knows it.

**Jobs:** Functional — save time, nothing complicated (*"Photoshop solutions are very
complicated"*). Emotional — be heard and recognised by management; a sense of *"do the job or
lose your position."* Social — Al-Hilal must always look perfect; that pressure is the source
of the time problem.

**Anxiety / habit:** *"A lot of startups knock the door but never come again."* His stated trust
tests: **adaptability, sustainability, education.** They need to be educated about new tools and
have no time to learn them.

**Buying:** Not his responsibility. Never recommended a tool — *"no time to research."*

**Disqualifier:** Player sales sit in a different department. **"They are game analysts, not
data analysts."**

**Referral offered:** PFSA and معهد إعداد القادة (Leaders Development Institute).

**Q7:** `Interview 1 – Performance analyst, elite club U18 academy: A full working day is
consumed per match by camera set-up, clipping and Photoshop, run single-handed, leaving no time
for analysis itself and no capacity to engage with data at all.`

**Q8:** `Interview 1 – Performance analyst: The user's job to be done is getting his day back
and being recognised by management, not better metrics. He is a game analyst, not a data
analyst, and could not evaluate advanced analytics if offered — so the product must deliver
conclusions rather than dashboards, and teach as it works.`

**Interest: MAYBE** — engaged, but not the buyer and unable to assess the core product.

# 2 — Performance analyst, Al-Ittifaq (USER)

**Pain:** Time, and lack of awareness of what is possible.

**What we learned:** They are fully occupied with **game analysis, not data**. They would value
something that **saves time and supports decision-making rather than adding work**. The product
was not shown, so his interest is in the problem, not the solution.

**Q7:** `Interview 2 – Performance analyst, Pro League club: The analysis team is fully consumed
by game-analysis output, with no time or awareness to move toward data, so anything new is
judged first on whether it adds to the workload.`

**Q8:** `Interview 2 – Performance analyst: Adoption in this role is governed by workload, not
capability. A solution that saves time and supports decisions will be accepted; one that adds a
step will be rejected regardless of how good the analysis is.`

**Interest: MAYBE** — product not presented.

# 3 — Assistant coach, Al-Ula (USER) ⭐ competitor sighting

**Pain:** **Coaches and assistants have no security of tenure** — they move between clubs
repeatedly, often several times.

**Current solution:** **A competitor has been in place for two years.** His verdict: it **lacks
accuracy and lacks support.**

**Reaction to our product:** liked the **UI/UX**, the **AI assistant**, and **Arabic-first**.

**Q7:** `Interview 3 – Assistant coach, club academy: They have used an analysis tool for two
years that they consider inaccurate and poorly supported, while the coaching staff themselves
change clubs frequently, so continuity of both tools and people is fragile.`

**Q8:** `Interview 3 – Assistant coach: An incumbent competitor exists and is disliked for
accuracy and support rather than price — which means displacement is possible, and that
reliability and service matter more than features. He responded most to the Arabic-first AI
assistant and the interface, not to the metrics.`

**Interest: YES**

**Strategic notes**
- **First competitor confirmed in the wild**, and it is beatable on accuracy and support — the
  two things our confidence-flagged output and service model are built for.
- **Staff churn cuts both ways:** it shortens the life of any single relationship, but a coach
  who likes the product carries it to his next club. Worth designing for.

**Interest: YES**

product**, not features — and that "no analysts to hire" is the load-bearing promise.

# 6 — Lizzie Fluke, Director of Performance (GOVERNANCE / expert) ⭐ strongest outcome

**Her view of the region:** The region is **not advanced yet** and **lacks knowledge**.
**Directors are often not capable in their positions.** The field operates **top-down when it
should be bottom-up.**

**Her background:** Performance analyst at Malmö FF; has a **GPS tracking solution and built an
app for it**.

**Reaction to our product:** shown the product, **loved it**, and **asked for a second interview
to test it.**

**Q7:** `Interview 6 – Director of Performance and sport-systems specialist (KSA/MENA): The
regional gap is knowledge and capability rather than technology — decision-makers frequently
lack the expertise their positions require, and systems are imposed top-down instead of being
built from delivery upward.`

**Q8:** `Interview 6 – Director of Performance: The barrier in this market is comprehension, not
product quality — which reframes education as part of the offering rather than as marketing.
She also challenged our buyer-first targeting: if directors lack capability, selling only to
the top may fail where building from the delivery layer upward would succeed.`

**Interest: YES** — requested a follow-up session to test the product.

**Strategic notes**
- **She contradicts Darrena's buyer-first ruling.** Darrena: prioritise whoever pays. Lizzie:
  directors are often not capable, and the system should be built bottom-up. **Both may be
  right** — sell to the buyer, but design for and demonstrate through the delivery layer.
  This tension belongs in Assignment #2.
- **She has a competing or complementary product** (GPS tracking + app). GPS measures physical
  load; we measure tactical decisions — historically complementary, not competitive. **Clarify
  which she intends before sharing anything further.**
- **The second session is the most valuable follow-up from all seven interviews.** Prepare it
  properly and decide in advance what to show and what to hold.

# 7 — Dr Mohammed Alzaydani, Sporting Director, Al-Najmah (BUYER) ⭐ only true buyer

**His world:** Sporting Director. PMP-certified; background includes dentistry. Profile
describes implementing data-driven decision-making, governance and regulatory compliance. Record
of promotions culminating in the Saudi Pro League.

**Current solution — what they actually track:** attendance, simple event data, injuries,
competing and minutes played across the **last 2–3 seasons**, consistency and mentality.
**Administrative and longitudinal — but nothing about performance on the pitch.**

**Pain:** No advanced solution. General records only; nothing targeted.

**Q7:** `Interview 7 – Sporting Director, Pro League club: Player decisions rest on general
records — attendance, minutes played, injuries, consistency and mentality across two to three
seasons — with no targeted performance data, so comparing two similar players comes down to
impression.`

**Q8:** `Interview 7 – Sporting Director: A club that genuinely describes itself as data-driven
still holds only administrative data on its players. The gap is not willingness or
sophistication — it is that nobody supplies targeted performance data. This places the
opportunity below the analysis department rather than inside it, and confirms the buyer
understands the value of evidence long before he understands the technology.`

**Interest: YES**

**Strategic note:** the most important finding in the set. **They already collect longitudinal
data across 2–3 seasons** — attendance, minutes, consistency. That is the skeleton of a player
development record with the performance layer missing. **The Verified Asset Ledger is not a new
behaviour we must teach; it is a completion of one they already have.**

# Cross-interview patterns

1. **Every club has game analysts. None has a data analyst.** Confirmed at Al-Hilal, Al-Ittifaq
   and Al-Najmah. *"They are game analysts, not data analysts."*
2. **Time is the user's pain; evidence is the buyer's.** Two value propositions, one product.
3. **The users cannot evaluate the differentiator.** Alaa could not engage with data at all. So
   the product must deliver conclusions, not dashboards — and the coach at Al-Ansar responding
   to notifications over metrics confirms it.
4. **No hire is possible.** Al-Hilal refused one; Al-Ansar's staff is fixed. Any solution
   requiring a person is dead on arrival, which makes the AI assistant load-bearing.
5. **Vendor longevity is a purchase criterion.** *"A lot of startups knock the door but never
   come again."* Trust tests: adaptability, sustainability, education.
6. **The data that exists is administrative, not performance** — attendance, minutes, injuries.
   Nothing about what happened on the pitch.
7. **The incumbent is beatable on accuracy and support**, not price (Al-Ula).
8. **Knowledge, not technology, is the regional barrier** (Lizzie) — so education is part of the
   product.
