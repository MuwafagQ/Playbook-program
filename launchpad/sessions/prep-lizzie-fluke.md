# Interview prep — Lizzie Fluke, Director of Performance
**Sunday morning.** Booked from LinkedIn outreach.

## Who she is — and what she is NOT
Director of Performance. Designs **integrated performance, medical and athlete pathway
systems**. Saudi Arabian Olympic Committee, Malmö FF, Etihad Club, London City Lionesses.
Has led academy and youth teams and national age groups. **Partners directly with Presidents,
Chief Executives, Boards and Technical Directors.** Pioneered Saudi's first multi-sport
Women's Olympic Wild Card Programme. Publishes and speaks on **governance, sport system
development and athlete pathways across Saudi-MENA.** Top skills listed: Sport Governance,
Sport Systems, High-Performance Sport.

**She is not a buyer.** She is an **expert informant, a systems architect, and a potential
door-opener** — closer to an industry authority than a customer.

**Treat her as the governance-segment interview** (slot 4 in the plan). That is legitimate and
it was always in the design.

### Why she may be the single most valuable conversation of Phase 1
She can answer the question nobody else has been able to: **is the Ministry governance mandate
real, what does it actually require, and who is accountable for it?** That assumption sits
underneath the pricing, the ICP and the buying trigger, and right now it rests on hearsay.

She also works with the exact people you cannot reach — **Presidents, CEOs, Boards, Technical
Directors** — and she publishes to a Saudi-MENA sport audience.

## How to pitch the conversation to her
Not "will you buy this." Frame it as **a system question from someone building inside the
system.** She writes about exactly this; she will engage with the question, not the product.

> "I'm building analytics for mid-league academies, and I keep hitting the same wall: these
> clubs have game analysts but no data capability, and the people who'd benefit can't
> interpret data. You design these systems for a living across MENA. I'd value your read on
> where something like this actually fits — or whether it doesn't."

## Speak her language
She quantifies in **system outcomes, not features**: 90%+ athlete availability across three
seasons; **£500K+ preserved annually** through evidence-led decisions and workload
optimisation; 45+ person cross-functional teams. She thinks in **availability, pathways,
governance, evidence-led decision making, value preserved.**

**Mirror that.** Your equivalent argument: *evidence-led technical and tactical decisions,
and a documented development pathway that preserves player value at transfer.* That is her
framing applied to your domain, and she will recognise it instantly.

## The 8 questions

| # | Question | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Tell me about the systems you've built in Saudi — what does a performance department actually look like at a club here?" | Open, flattering, and maps the org chart you've been guessing at |
| 2 | **"Where does technical and tactical analysis sit in that structure? Who owns it, and who does it report to?"** | Your product's home in the org — you don't know this |
| 3 | **"You work with Boards and Presidents. When they ask whether the academy is working, what evidence do they actually accept?"** | The buyer's real decision criteria, from someone who sits in those rooms |
| 4 | **"There's a Ministry requirement for clubs to implement data and analytics tools. How real is that, what does it require, and who's accountable for it?"** | ⭐ **THE question.** Your entire trigger hypothesis |
| 5 | "You've preserved £500K a year through evidence-led decisions on the medical side. Is there an equivalent argument on the technical side, or does the money not work the same way?" | Uses her own number; tests your ROI story against an expert |
| 6 | "At the mid-league and academy level — is there anyone who works with data, or is it all game analysts?" | Validates the ICP gap that Alaa suggested |
| 7 | **"What have you seen fail when someone brings technology into these environments?"** | Your anxiety and habit forces, from someone who's watched it happen repeatedly |
| 8 | **"Who should I be speaking to? And would you be open to looking at what we've built and telling me where it's wrong?"** | The ask — introductions and a standing advisory relationship |

## Two things to be careful about
1. **Her background is medical, physical and system-level, not tactical analytics.** Tactical
   video may sit outside her remit. **Ask, don't assume** (Q2). If it does sit outside, that
   is itself a finding: it tells you which department owns your budget.
2. **Do not pitch.** She is an educator and a publisher; she will engage with a good question
   and disengage from a sales call. Keep the Mom Test discipline exactly as with everyone else.

## The ask at the end
Q8 is the point of the call. Two specific things:
- **Introductions** to Technical Directors or academy leadership in her network
- **Would she look at the product and tell you what's wrong with it?** A senior advisor who
  publishes in this market is worth more than a customer at this stage — for credibility, for
  the deck, and for Phase 4.

Do not ask her to be an advisor formally on the first call. Ask for a reaction. Earn the rest.
