## Playbook-IQ
**A science lab and data department for football clubs and academies.**

We turn ordinary broadcast match footage into tactical intelligence — no cameras, no hardware
installed at the ground, and no data analyst to hire.

Based in Saudi Arabia. Currently in the **MISK Launchpad accelerator**, on the **Ministry of
Sport SportTech track**.

---

## The problem we're addressing

From our own customer research across Saudi clubs and academies — analysts, coaches, scouts,
a sporting director and a performance systems specialist:

- **Every club has game analysts. Almost none has a data analyst.** Analysis is done on video
  and graphics tools; the data layer beneath it simply doesn't exist.
- **A single match consumes a full working day** of set-up, clipping and manual editing —
  usually run by one person. Most matches are never analysed properly.
- **Hiring is not the answer, because hiring isn't possible.** Clubs we spoke to had repeatedly
  requested additional analysis staff and been refused.
- **Player decisions rest on administrative records** — attendance, minutes played, injuries,
  consistency — with nothing describing what actually happened on the pitch.

The gap is not ambition or budget. It is that **nobody supplies the performance data**, and
nobody at these clubs has the time or training to produce it themselves.

---

## What we do

**We are the data layer beneath the analysis department** — not a replacement for it.

Coaches and analysts keep doing their work. We supply the structured tactical intelligence they
have never had access to, delivered as conclusions rather than as dashboards, in Arabic and
English, with no interpretation required and no new hire.

**Three things define the approach:**

**Curation over volume.** Most platforms bury clubs in numbers. Our work is separating the
metrics that matter from the noise — a small, carefully chosen set, developed with sports
science partners.

**Conclusions, not dashboards.** The product tells a coach what happened and what was wrong
with it, in a sentence he can act on. It requires no analytics vocabulary.

**Arabic-first, built for this region.** Not a translated interface — a product designed for how
clubs here actually work.

## Who it's for

Private academies and mid-league clubs across the GCC that develop and sell players — the
segment priced out of building a data department, and underserved by platforms designed for
Tier-1 European budgets.

Secondary: scouting operations and player representation, where evidence of development carries
direct commercial value.

# ⚠ WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT IN THIS DOCUMENT

Check this list before adding anything to a version you send out.

**Never include in an external one-pager:**
- The **momentum and counter-attack metric family** — the proprietary work. Do not name it, do
  not describe what it measures, do not show a screenshot containing it.
- **Tactical phase labels and notification wording** (the in-match event detection). The words
  themselves describe the concept.
- **Any technical architecture** — the pipeline, models, tracking approach, what is automated
  versus assisted, the re-identification position.
- **Pricing**, tiers, or usage limits.
- **Named interviewees or their clubs.** "A Pro League academy analyst" is fine; a name is not.
- **Named competitors** or what we know about their weaknesses.
- **Anything about what the product cannot yet do.** True, necessary internally, and not for a
  document you cannot control the circulation of.

**Safe to include, and strong:**
- The market problem, because it is evidenced by our own research and is not proprietary.
- The positioning — curation, conclusions over dashboards, Arabic-first, no hire required.
- Credentials: MISK, the Ministry of Sport track, Barça Innovation Hub.
- The pilot offer — it is a concrete ask and it costs us nothing to make.

**Rule of thumb:** this document should make someone want a conversation. It should not let
them build anything, and it should not let them assess our weaknesses.
