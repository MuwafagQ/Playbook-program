03 — Corrections

Four things we believed that were wrong

Each of these was written down as fact and has now been fixed in the repository. Listing them is not self-flagellation — it is the difference between a plan and a wish.

“A team-level tier runs automatically today, no Re-ID needed”

Was: the basis of the whole MVP proposal.

Wrong. Those metrics need a tactical camera. We have broadcast only. There is no free automated tier, and Re-ID is must-have, not a premium upsell — the mentor was right, and clubs have already rejected a competitor over unstable IDs.

“Our footage is an asset we can trade to a research lab”

Was: the opening pitch to KAUST.

Wrong. The Ministry platform is open to every company. What we can offer a lab is club access and a real deployment site — not data.

“Clubs already buy event feeds, so sell metrics on top”

Was: a proposed zero-cost wedge.

Wrong for our market. Wyscout and Opta do not cover grassroots. There is no feed to plug into. Tagging by hand is the only source.

“Only 34 startups reach the funding stage”

Was: in the program timeline CSV.

Wrong. The Ministry of Sport deck states the Phase 2 filtration as 125 → 50. The original recollection of 50 was right. SportTech track: 1000+ applications, 95+ SportTech, 31 onboarded.

04 — The reframe

Sell the metric layer, not the pipeline

The product is the metric layer. How the data underneath is produced is an implementation detail that can change from manual, to tracking, to full automation without the customer ever noticing.

This is the move that makes the situation survivable. It works because the metrics that define the product are mostly event-based — so hand-tagged Layer 1 delivers the real product, slowly, rather than a mock-up of it. Re-ID stops being the thing that blocks the business and becomes the thing that scales it.

And the timing fits. A youth coach needs the analysis before the next training session — roughly 48 hours. Twelve person-hours fits inside that. We are not hiding a broken promise; we are meeting the one that matters.

LayerHow data is producedStatus
1 — EventsFounder + Data Operations Specialist tag ball events and positional notes by handNow · ~12 person-hours per match
2 — TrackingAutomated tracking with stable identitiesBlocked on Re-ID · research track via KAUST
3 — AutomationEnd to end, unattendedTriggered by demand, at customer #4

SPL coaches use data to win. Youth coaches use it to develop. That sentence is the sharpest positioning we have, and it carries a product consequence: development is invisible in a single match. A one-match pilot under-sells us. Only a trend across three or four fixtures tests the actual claim.

05 — Priorities

What the next five weeks are for

1

Run one pilot: 3–4 matches, one youth team

Delivered before each next training session, at Layer 1. It is the only thing here that produces the evidence seven interviews did not. Roughly 36–48 person-hours — it fits.

2

Name a price to a decision-maker and record the reaction

It does not need to be the right number. If October ends without one real reaction to one real price, we finish the month with the same hole we started it with.

3

Open KAUST in week one

Academic calendars are slow. The ask is a collaboration — a deployment site and club access in exchange for work on Re-ID under broadcast conditions — not a request for help.

4

Measure our own failure rate

ID switches per player per minute, percentage of frames with good homography, mean identity duration before fragmentation. A few hours of compute. It converts the KAUST ask from “we have gaps” into “here is exactly where we break.”

5

Log human hours per match, every match

This single number sets what turnaround we can promise and what price is survivable. Without it, pricing is guesswork dressed as strategy.

07 — Exposure

Open risks, including the ones nobody raised

Unresolved, raised repeatedly
  • The repository is public and contains the risk register, named interviewees with their clubs, and the candid technical assessment. Raised three times, still open.
  • The dashboard front-end source is in no version control anywhere. One laptop failure loses it.
Commercial
  • Pricing is entirely unvalidated — ~30k SAR/yr was “what feels payable,” and grassroots budgets are far below pro clubs
  • Manual delivery has labour cost, not compute cost — a learning phase, not a model
  • Once a club sees us as a manual service, repositioning is hard. The lab framing survives all three layers; “automated platform” does not
  • StepOut.ai: two years in and still failing on ID stability. We cannot claim superiority we have not demonstrated
Legal & structural
  • Grassroots means minors. Individual analysis on under-18s, shared with coaches and parents, sits under PDPL. Academies will ask — an answer ready is a credibility asset
  • Ministry footage permission is verbal, and the commercial-use question is unanswered
  • Royal Decree M/121 — registry transition to 11 June 2027. A real trigger, but it mandates registration, not analytics purchases
  • Effectively a solo founder with one part-time non-programmer
08 — The one sentence

If you read nothing else

We cannot ship automation by 30 October, by any route — so we should stop trying, deliver the real product by hand to one youth team across four matches, and find out whether anyone will pay for it. Re-ID is the scaling problem, and it belongs to a research partner, not to five weeks of founder time.
