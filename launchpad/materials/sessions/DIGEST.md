## ⭐ 1. The MVP Scope Canvas — the framework Assignment #2 is built on
Zohare Haider. *"Keep cutting until only the question is left."*

**Six cells, one page:**
| # | Cell | Test of a good answer |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **One user, named** | A person you could email tonight. "Mid-market academies" fails; "the operations manager of a 3–8 branch restaurant group in Riyadh" passes. |
| 02 | **One job, their words** | No product name in it. "I want to know on Monday what last week actually cost me." |
| 03 | **Riskiest assumption** | The one that, if wrong, kills the company. *"We assume they will pay rather than keep using the spreadsheet."* |
| 04 | **Smallest killing test** | Cheapest way to be proven wrong. *"Ten paid pilots at any price, run by hand, no product at all."* |
| 05 | **Kill number and date** | Written **before** the test runs. *"Fewer than 4 of 10 convert by 31 October and we stop."* |
| 06 | **The not-list** | What you refuse to build. *"No mobile app. No integrations. No second country. Not this quarter."* |

**"Cells 3 and 5 do the work. They are the ones you are most likely to skip."**

His own failure, told against himself: he skipped 3 and 5, then lost 6 — built a POS integration because one customer asked loudly, opened a second market before the first paid for itself, and spent **11 months building before the first honest no.** *"Every line of it worked. None of it answered the question."*

**Three ways scope climbs back in:** an investor question that becomes a roadmap item by Friday; one loud customer turning you into a consultancy with a single client; and (p18 continues) the third.

**For us:** this is the spine of Assignment #2. Our cell 03 is almost certainly *"we assume a club will pay rather than continue deciding on impression"* — and we have **zero pricing evidence after seven interviews**, which is exactly the assumption he says kills companies. Cell 04 is the one-match pilot offer. Cell 05 we have never written.

## ⭐ 2. Pricing & Activation Models — our biggest gap, directly addressed

**Three pricing strategies**
| Strategy | Pro | Con |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost-plus** | simple, guarantees profit | ignores the customer and the competition |
| **Value-based** | perception, loyalty, bigger profits | smaller market, more competition |
| **Competitor-based** | low risk, straightforward | unsustainable, incomplete information |

*"Price without a purpose = a guess."* Their three questions before pricing: **what single metric must pricing improve now? which ICP are we pricing for? does pricing fit our positioning?**

**Eight pricing models:** flat rate · usage-based · **tier-based** · user-based · **outcome-based** · feature-based · credit-based · **hybrid**.

Relevant to us:
- **Tier-based** — appeals to multiple customer types, allows upsells; but tiers confuse if badly named and anchored. *This is our current basic/premium structure.*
- **Usage-based** — scales with usage, but customers cannot predict their bill and may be discouraged from using the product. *This is our 4-matches-a-month cap and the KEMO token metering — and the "discouraged from using it" warning matters for a product whose whole pitch is "analyse every match."*
- **Outcome-based** — revenue tied to a core KPI. Pros: revenue equals customer success, built-in expansion, signals confidence. Cons: measurement complexity, revenue volatility, higher vendor risk. *Worth considering against player-sale value — but measurement complexity is real.*
- **Hybrid** — differentiation and customisation, at the cost of complexity.

**Activation models — "the right pricing model with the wrong activation model can result in product failure."**
- **Freemium** — good when the product gets more valuable the more it is used and when users are beginners. Low free-to-paid conversion.
- **Free trial** — 3/7/14/30 days, card or no card.
- **Reverse trial** — a freemium account that auto-unlocks all paid features for 7–14 days, then falls back. Zero card friction, data-driven upsell, built-in fallback.
- **Request a demo.**

**For us:** our one-match pilot offer is effectively a **free trial with no card**. A **reverse trial** may fit better — give a club everything for two weeks on real footage, then fall back to the base tier. It matches a market where, per our interviews, buyers will not commit before seeing output on their own match.

## 3. Understanding Your Market — Sam Akdere

*"Healthcare is an industry. It is not your market."*

**Market = Customer + Problem + Context.** Example: *"Private clinics losing revenue because patients miss appointments."*

**Narrow like this:** Everyone → Industry → Segment → Specific customer → Specific problem.

**Look for customers who:** have a real problem · have a reason to solve it **now** · can actually buy.

**Market sizing formula: target customers × annual spend.** *"3,000 clinics × $6,000/year = $18M."*
*"The healthcare software market is worth $10 billion" — **so what?***

Three questions: are there enough customers? can they pay enough? can I reach them at reasonable cost?

**For us:** this is exactly the bottom-up SOM we already use (600 academies × ACV). It validates the method **and** warns against the top-down 54.3B SAR TAM figure in our deck, which is the "so what" number.

## 4. Competitive Analysis — Sam Akdere

*"If the problem exists, customers are already doing something."*

**Three competitor types:** **Direct** (same problem, similar way) · **Indirect** (same problem, differently) · **Status quo** (what they do today).

**Better ≠ worth switching.** Switching costs: time, money, risk, learning, integration.
The question is not *"why is our product better?"* but **"why would someone switch to us?"**

**They say vs. customers say:** website, deck, marketing versus reviews, complaints, interviews.

**A useful gap is:** important to the customer · poorly solved today · something you can actually solve.
*"Competitor has 12 features → we need 13"* is the wrong instinct.

**Their homework:** find 3–5 alternatives, read 20 reviews, speak to 3 customers about what they use today, identify the biggest switching barrier, write one reason to switch.

**For us:** our competitive map now has real entries — **direct: StepOut.ai** (two years inside a Saudi academy, weak on CV accuracy and support) · **indirect: Hudl, Metrica, telestration tools, GPS/wearables** · **status quo: a game analyst with video and Photoshop, and doing nothing.** Our useful gap is exactly their test: important, poorly solved, and solvable by us.

## 5. Sprints — Walid Elaghil (our own mentor)

*"'Sprint' is borrowed from a world you don't live in."* Scrum assumes a PM, an engineering team, a Scrum Master, 6–10 people. **In a startup, every one of those roles is you.** Keep the discipline, drop the ceremony.

**The loop: Plan → Build → Reflect. Three moves, every seven days.** Plus a Sprint Canvas, the plan ritual ("where sprints are actually won or lost"), sprinting with AI, and common mistakes.

His framing question: *"What did you promise yourself you'd finish last week — and did you?"* **Tasks vs. outcomes.**

**For us:** he is our assigned mentor, so using his framework in Assignment #2's roadmap is both sound and politically sensible. A weekly Plan → Build → Reflect rhythm is also the right cadence for a solo founder with one part-time colleague.

## ⭐ 8. Ministry of Sport — the partner session

### National Sports Strategy (NSS)
*"An effective sports sector that builds an active society and attains international recognition."*

- **Core pillars:** elite performance · **talent development pathway** · mass participation
- **Sports delivery enablers:** facilities · human capital · events and engagement
- **Sports ecosystem enablers:** **communication & digitisation** · sports economy · **federation and club support** · **regulation & governance**
- **Guiding principles:** diversity and inclusion · merit-based sector · integrity

**Playbook-IQ maps onto three of these by name: talent development pathway, communication & digitisation, and regulation & governance.** Use their vocabulary in anything that reaches the Ministry.

### The ecosystem
8 core sporting organisations: **LDI** (Leaders Development Institute), SFA, **SOPC**, MAHD Academy, Federations, Associations/Leagues, **Sport Clubs**, Event Authorities.
3 government sectoral leads: Ministry of Education, Ministry of Municipalities and Housing, Ministry of Health.

### Investment Deputyship — relevant to us
Under Strategic Planning & Investment sit: **Investment Enablement** (frameworks, structured approach) · **Clubs Investment** (enabling clubs to activate assets) · **Privatization** (privatising public sports clubs, developing privatisation roadmaps) · **Portfolio Investments** · **Partnerships** (commercialisation).

**This is the department behind the club privatisation we saw at Al-Ula.** It is also the natural home for any funding-mechanism conversation.

There is also a **Deputyship of Digital Transformation**.
