# Interview guide — one page, hold this on the call
**25 minutes. Target 4–6 completed by Tue 15 Sep.** Assignment #1 requires a minimum of 6.

## The three rules
1. **Never pitch.** If you describe the product, the interview is contaminated and the data
   is worthless. If they ask what you're building, say *"I'll show you at the end — I don't
   want to bias your answers"* and move on.
2. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "The last time you…" not "would you…". People are
   accurate about what they did and unreliable about what they would do.
3. **Silence is a tool.** After they answer, wait. The second half of the answer is the
   valuable half.

Ask permission to record — Assignment #1 asks for *proof* of customer discovery.

---

## 0. Open (2 min)
> "Thank you for the time. I'm doing research, not selling — I want to understand how you
> actually work with match video. There are no right answers. Do you mind if I record this for
> my own notes?"

## 1. Their world (5 min)
1. Tell me about your role — what are you responsible for?
2. Walk me through the last match you analysed. What did you actually do?
3. Who else was involved? Who saw the output?
4. How long did it take?

## 2. Current tools & spend (5 min)
5. What tools do you use for that today?
6. What do they cost per year?
7. Who on staff works with data, as opposed to video?
8. Have you looked at Hudl, Metrica, or anything similar? What happened?
9. What have you tried and stopped using? Why did you stop?

## 3. The pain (5 min)
10. What's the most frustrating part of that whole process?
11. What do you wish you knew about a match that you currently can't find out?
12. When was the last time you made a decision — selection, tactics, a signing — and wished
    you'd had better information?
13. What happens if you just… don't analyse a match? Who notices?

## 4. Buying (4 min)
14. If you wanted to bring in a new tool, what would that process look like?
15. Who would need to approve it? Who else would have an opinion?
16. What would have to be true for your academy to buy something like that this season?
17. *(Only if they raise it — don't lead)* Is there anything the Ministry or the federation
    requires you to have in place around data and analytics?

## 5. Money — LAST, and only after they've described real pain (3 min)
18. If something solved [repeat their exact words back], what would you expect it to cost?
19. Would that be bought per team, or once for the whole academy?
20. Who signs off on that number?

**Never say your price first.** If they ask, deflect once: *"I'm still working that out —
what would feel right to you?"* Then let it go. You have never quoted anyone; these six
conversations are your first real pricing data.

## 6. Close (1 min)
21. Is there anything I should have asked you and didn't?
22. **Who else should I be talking to? Could you introduce me to two or three people?**
23. Would it be useful if I came back and showed you what I've built?

Q22 is how six interviews become twelve. Ask it every single time.

---

## Optional, at the very end only
If it's gone well and they're curious, show the 3-minute demo — and **lead with the
notification firing**, not the metrics table. Then ask: *"What would you do with that?"*
Their unprompted answer is worth more than any feature list.

## After every interview — within 10 minutes
Write down, while it's fresh:
- Their exact words for the problem (verbatim quotes — these become the assignment)
- What they use today and what it costs
- Who they said signs
- Any number they said out loud
- Surprises — anything that contradicted what you expected

The surprises are the whole point. If nothing surprised you, you were pitching.
