# Interview guide — BUYERS (directors, owners, sporting directors)
**25–30 min. 8 core questions, plus a bank to draw from.** For interviews 4, 5 and 6. The user-side guide is `interview-guide.md`.

## Why this is a different conversation
A director does not analyse matches. He decides about **people, money and risk**. So the
"tell me about the last time" stories are not about workflow — they are about **decisions**:
the last player he sold, the last time he had to justify something to the board, the last
tool the academy bought.

**The three things this interview must establish:**
1. Does he actually feel a pain here, or is this his analyst's problem and not his?
2. Is this in his **top three priorities**, or is it number seven? (Darrena's filter 2 — if
   it's #7 you will never close, no matter how good the product is.)
3. Who signs, and what does the buying process really look like?

## The rules (same as user interviews)
- **Never pitch.** Generative interview only. If you want to show the dashboard, close the
  interview first and say so out loud.
- **Ask about the past, not the future.** Especially about money.
- **Allow silence.** Directors are used to being deferred to; the pause is where the honest
  answer comes.
- **Try to hear things you don't want to hear.** A director saying "this isn't a priority for
  us" is the most valuable sentence you can get this week.
- With a highly credentialled counterpart, **defer**: *"You know this better than I do — that's
  why I'm asking."*

# THE 8 QUESTIONS — this is the actual interview
**25 minutes = ~20 minutes of questions. A real answer plus its follow-ups takes 2–3 minutes.
That is 7–8 questions, not 36.** The long list further down is a *bank* to draw from when a
thread opens or an answer runs dry — not a script to work through.

**Memorise these eight. Everything else is optional.**

| # | Question | What it gets you |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Tell me about your role — what are you actually responsible for?" | Warm-up, and whether he holds budget |
| 2 | **"What's taking up most of your attention this season?"** | Priority ranking. If analytics doesn't come up unprompted, that is your finding |
| 3 | **"Walk me through the last player who left your academy. How did you make the case for what he was worth?"** | The Verified Asset Ledger, tested against a real event |
| 4 | "When the board asks why the team is performing the way it is, what do you show them?" | The evidence gap, and the emotional job |
| 5 | "Who works with match video today, and what do they produce?" | Current solution and the habit |
| 6 | **"What would have to happen for you to make this a top priority next month?"** | The trigger — the "why now" |
| 7 | **"What was the last thing the academy bought that cost real money? How did that get decided, and who signed?"** | Budget signal and the signer, from past behaviour |
| 8 | **"Would you let us analyse one of your matches so you can see what comes out?"** | Commitment. If yes, put a date in the diary during the call |

**Then always:** *"Who else should I be speaking to? Could you introduce me to two people?"*

## How to actually run it
- **Ask the question, then stop talking.** The pause is where the real answer lives.
- **Follow every answer with "why?" or "tell me more about that."** Three levels deep on one
  good answer beats eight questions skimmed.
- **Follow the energy.** If he starts telling you about a player sale that went badly, abandon
  the order and stay there. That is the interview.
- **Print the eight on one page.** Do not read from a laptop — Katya's rule.
- If the conversation runs long and he's engaged, pull more from the bank below. If he's short
  on time, **questions 3, 6 and 7 are the ones you cannot leave without.**

# THE QUESTION BANK
*Reference only. Pull from these when a thread opens. Do not work through them in order.*

## 0. Open (2 min)
> "Thank you for the time. I'm doing research, not selling — I want to understand how
> academies at your level actually run and where the real pressure sits. There are no right
> answers, and nothing you say goes anywhere with your name on it. Do you mind if I take
> notes?"

## 1. Their world and what they're measured on (5 min)
`[→ ICP: Who]` `[→ JTBD: Context]`
1. Tell me about your role — what are you actually responsible for?
2. How many teams and players sit under you?
3. **What does a good season look like for you? How is that judged, and by whom?**
4. Who do you report to — a board, an owner, a federation?
5. What's taking up most of your attention this season?

*Q5 is a soft version of "what's in your top three." Listen for whether anything analytics-
adjacent appears unprompted. If it doesn't, that is a finding.*

## 2. The player-development and valuation story (6 min)
`[→ ICP: Pain]` `[→ JTBD: Push]` `[→ tests the Verified Asset Ledger directly]`
6. **Walk me through the last player who left your academy — sold, promoted or moved on.
   How did that conversation go?**
7. How did you make the case for what he was worth?
8. What did you have to show the buying club? What did they ask for that you didn't have?
9. When you decide a player is progressing well — what are you actually going on?
10. **Tell me about a time you were wrong about a player.** What would have told you sooner?

*Q6–8 are the sharpest questions in this interview. If he has no story here, the Verified
Asset Ledger has no buyer at this academy — write that down.*

## 3. Decisions and evidence (4 min)
`[→ JTBD: functional / emotional / social]`
11. When the board or the owner asks why the team is performing the way it is, what do you
    show them?
12. Has anyone ever challenged a decision of yours — selection, a signing, a coach — and what
    did you use to defend it?
13. What do the parents ask you for?
14. What do the academies you compare yourself to have, that you don't?

*Q12 is the emotional job — fear of blame. Q13 and Q14 are the social job — reputation and
standing. Directors rarely say these directly; hear them in the tone.*

## 4. What exists today (4 min)
`[→ ICP: Current Solution]` `[→ JTBD: Habit]` `[→ Value prop: Unlike]`
15. Who on your staff works with match video? What do they produce, and who reads it?
16. Do you have anyone who works with **data**, as opposed to video?
17. What tools does the academy pay for today?
18. Have you looked at Hudl, Metrica or anything similar? What happened?
19. **What have you tried in this area that didn't work out?** Why did it stop?

*Q19 matters: a failed internal attempt is one of the strongest "why now" triggers there is.*

## 5. The trigger (3 min)
`[→ ICP: Trigger]` `[→ the highest-value question in the interview]`
20. **"What would have to happen for you to make this a top priority next month?"**
21. Has anything changed recently — a new mandate, new funding, a new owner, a target you've
    been given?
22. *(Only if he raises it — never lead)* Is there anything the Ministry or the federation
    requires you to have in place around data and analytics? What does that involve?

## 6. Money — past behaviour only (4 min)
`[→ ICP: Budget Signal]` `[→ pricing validation]`
23. **What was the last thing the academy bought that cost real money? How did that decision
    get made?**
24. Who signs off on something like that? Does it go to a board?
25. Is there a budget line for tools and technology? Roughly what scale?
26. **Have you paid for anything like this before — analysis, scouting data, software? What
    did it cost?**
27. When in the year do those decisions get made?

**Never name your price.** If he asks: *"I'm still working that out — what have you seen
things like this priced at?"* Then stop talking.

*Q27 is quietly important: if budgets are set once a season, you need to know the window.*

## 7. Buying process (3 min)
`[→ ICP: who signs]`
28. If you decided you wanted something like this, what would actually have to happen?
29. Who else would need to be convinced? Who could block it?
30. How long would that realistically take?
31. Would that be bought once for the academy, or per team?

*Q31 tests the per-team pricing assumption directly. It is currently a guess.*

## 8. Close with commitment (3 min)
`[→ Form Q9–11: yes / maybe / no]`
32. Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
33. **"Would you let us analyse one of your matches, so you can see what comes out?"**
    — if yes, **put a date in the diary during the call.** A yes with no date is a maybe.
34. **"If it were useful, is this something the academy would pay for?"**
35. **"Who else should I be speaking to? Could you introduce me to two people?"**
36. If he is a technical director rather than the signer: **"Would you introduce me to whoever
    holds the budget?"**

**Applause is not validation.** Score on what he committed to — a date, a name, a number.

## If he turns out not to be the buyer
Common — a technical director often influences rather than signs. That is not a wasted
interview: he becomes your **champion**, and Q36 is then the most valuable question you ask.
Record him as a user-side interview and keep hunting for the signer.
