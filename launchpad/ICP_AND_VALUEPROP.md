## Part 1 — ICP Hypothesis (Darrena's template)

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Private football academies in the GCC, ~150–600 registered players, running multiple age-group teams (U18/U16/U14), **that develop and sell players.** Buyer: owner or technical director. |
| **Pain** | They make selection, tactical and player-valuation decisions from opinion. They have game analysts but no data capability, and cannot prove a player's progression to a buying club. Building a data department costs ~600k SAR/year — impossible for them. |
| **Current solution** | Hudl, Metrica, telestration tools, and mostly nothing. Video watched by a game analyst; no structured data. |
| **Trigger ("Why Now")** | **The Saudi Ministry of Sport grants funding to certain academies and requires them to implement advanced data-analytics tools; compliance is financially rewarded and audited.** Secondary triggers: an upcoming player sale, a new technical director, a failed attempt at an internal analytics effort. |
| **Budget signal** | Ministry grant money earmarked for exactly this. Existing spend on video tools. Salaries paid to game analysts. |
| **Success metric** | Compliance satisfied; better-evidenced selection decisions; a defensible number attached to a player at transfer. |
| **Disqualifiers** | Academies that don't sell players (no Ledger value). Clubs with an existing data department (we replace nothing). Tier-1 pro clubs (long procurement, custom demands — the wishlist ICP, not the reality ICP). Academies with no video at all. |

### The three filters (Darrena, slide 5)
- **Pain?** Yes for mandated academies — costly, recurring, externally enforced. **Unverified** for the rest; that's what the buyer interviews must establish.
- **Willing to pay?** Budget exists where the grant applies. **Whether this ranks in their top 3 priorities is unknown.** The key open question.
- **Will they switch?** Low switching cost — most have no incumbent to leave. This is our strongest filter and worth saying out loud.

### Trigger-based list building (Darrena, slide 6)
Triggered buyers close 3–5× faster. **Our trigger is the governance mandate.** So prioritize
academies that have recently received Ministry funding, announced a digital or performance
initiative, or hired a technical director in the last year. See
`outreach/linkedin-buyer-search.md`.

### Honest note on volume
Darrena: 20–30 interviews, patterns emerge after 15+, under 10 is anecdote. **Assignment #1
requires 6.** We will do 6–8 well and label the ICP a *hypothesis*, not a finding. Saying that
explicitly in the assignment is more credible than overclaiming from six conversations.

## Part 2 — Value Proposition (Sherzod's framework)

### Primary — the BUYER (academy owner / technical director)

> **For** private football academies in the GCC that develop and sell players
> **Who want** to prove their players' progression and satisfy the Ministry's data
> requirements, without building a data department
> **Playbook-IQ is** a science lab and data department, delivered as software
> **That** turns ordinary broadcast match footage into a curated set of tactical metrics and
> a verified record of every player's development — no cameras, no hardware, no analysts to hire
> **Unlike** Hudl and telestration tools, which give you video to watch and still require
> someone who knows what to look for; and unlike Catapult and GPS vests, which measure
> physical load but not decisions — we tell you *what happened and what was wrong with it*,
> in Arabic, in a sentence a coach can act on.

### Secondary — the USER (analyst / coach)

> **For** analysts and coaches at academies with no data department
> **Who want** to know what actually happened in a match without spending a day on it
> **Playbook-IQ is** an automated match analysis platform
> **That** watches the match and tells them, in plain language, where their team lost control
> and why
> **Unlike** Hudl, which hands them footage and leaves the analysis to them

### Now vs. future (Sherzod, slide 17)
| Now | Future |
|---|---|
| A day of an analyst's time per match, and only the matches they can get to | Every match analysed, output the same day |
| Insight limited to what one person notices while watching | A curated metric set applied identically to every match |
| ~600k SAR/year to build a real data department | A fraction of that, no hires, no hardware |
| Player value argued from opinion at transfer | A verified record of progression |
| Analysis in English, built for data scientists | Arabic-native, built for coaches |

### The test (Sherzod, slide 7)
*"If a competitor could say the same sentence, it is not a value proposition."*
Hudl **cannot** say: no analyst required, Arabic-native tactical notifications, a brokered
science-lab layer for custom metrics, and a per-team price a mid-league academy can afford.
That's the defensible core.

## Open questions the interviews must answer
- [ ] Does the pain rank in their top 3 priorities, or is it #7?
- [ ] Is the governance mandate real, and how widely does it apply?
- [ ] Who actually signs — owner, technical director, or governance?
- [ ] What do they pay today, for anything analysis-related?
- [ ] Would they buy per team or once for the academy?
- [ ] **Would they buy an ugly, unpolished version?** (Darrena's ICP test — the sharpest
      question we can ask, and we can genuinely ask it, because ours *is* unpolished.)
